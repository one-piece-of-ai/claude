## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Call revised: back to LATE upcycle — TURN CALL SUSPENDED (not confirmed). Confidence: medium.** The single most important thing that changed this week: **all three conditions the 8/1 report set as its own "push-back-toward-plain-LATE" trigger fired at once.** (1) Semis didn't just hold — they **ripped +9.2% (SOX 11,311→12,356.79)**, the strongest week of the drawdown, cutting the peak-to-here loss from −22.8% to **−15.7%**; (2) the **SOX/SPX ratio reclaimed leadership**, rising 1.510→**1.593** as semis beat the S&P by ~5.6 points; and (3) the **hawkish-Fed scare that drove the equity turn reversed outright** — Friday's July jobs report showed the economy **lost 23,000 jobs** with **−146k of prior-month revisions**, collapsing rate-**hike** odds (Sept hike now ~44%, a *cut* back on the table per Barclays/Goldman) and pulling Treasury yields down. So the multiple-compression leg that broke the chip stocks in late July un-broke. **But three caveats keep this a suspension, not an all-clear:** the rally was **macro/rate-driven risk-on**, not semi-specific conviction; AMD (8/4) still **sold its beat** (−8% AH on a record DC quarter), so the "sell-the-good-news" micro-pattern isn't fully dead; and the real arbiter, **NVDA (Aug 26)**, hasn't reported. Meanwhile the *physical* cycle stayed unambiguously hot — **June WSTS re-accelerated to +9.7% MoM / +123.6% YoY**, DRAM/NAND spot kept climbing. Net: the equity-turn signal was invalidated-for-now by a macro override; whether that's a durable resumption or a bear-market bounce is NVDA's call.

## 3. KEY SIGNALS INTERPRETATION

- **Demand momentum: still accelerating in the cycle AND, this week, in the stocks — the 8/1 divergence narrowed.** The cleanest bear-signal of the last three weeks (*the best news can't lift the shares*) did not fire this week; instead semis rallied +9.2% and the SOX/SPX ratio turned up. Crucially, though, the trigger was **macro**, not fundamental re-acceptance: a weak jobs print collapsed the rate-hike scare and sparked a broad risk-on melt-up that lifted everything, with the highest-beta cohort (semis) leading. The June WSTS re-acceleration (+9.7% MoM) confirms the physical demand curve is still bending up — but a single macro-driven up-week is not proof the *equity* momentum re-based; it's proof the multiple headwind lifted.
- **Supply tightness vs. oversupply: tighter still — now bleeding into commodity DRAM.** The most notable new data point: **DDR4 spot (~$2.10/Gbit) has risen above HBM3e contract (~$1.70/Gbit)**, and mainstream DDR4 spot rose +3.31% in a single week. The shortage is no longer just an HBM/leading-edge story — it's pulling conventional/legacy DRAM up as makers divert capacity to AI. Samsung/SK Hynix's 2027+ shortage warnings stand. No oversupply anywhere in the data.
- **Memory cycle direction: physical shortage intact and broadening; price momentum still positive; equities bounced.** The trichotomy from 8/1 (fundamentals bullish / price-momentum cresting / stocks turned) partly re-converged this week as the memory names (SK Hynix, Sandisk/NAND) led the rally. Physical undersupply reaffirmed; the "stocks turned" leg is the one that reversed. Still late-cycle in character — prices high and rising, shortage extended — but the equity roll-over is on hold.
- **AI-driven demand strength vs. cyclical weakness: demand unambiguously strong; the "weakness" was always valuation+macro, and macro just flipped supportive.** AMD's DC revenue +107% to $6.7B, guide to $13B in Q3, and "DC doubles in 2027"; Supermicro's record >$60B backlog; June sales +123.6% YoY — the demand side has no cracks. The late-July weakness was multiple compression from a hawkish-Fed scare, and that scare inverted this week (weak labor → dovish repricing). The double edge to watch: a *cooling labor market* is bullish for multiples now but a forward demand risk if it deepens.

## 5. CYCLE POSITIONING CALL

- **Where we are: LATE upcycle — TURN CALL SUSPENDED (reverted from "TURNING" per the 8/1 report's own invalidation logic).** The 8/1 report specified that if semis held, reclaimed the SOX/SPX ratio and leadership, and the hawkish-Fed scare faded, the drawdown should re-read as a deep valuation reset inside an intact upcycle. **That is exactly what happened** — so intellectual honesty requires stepping the equity-turn call back. This is not a promotion to "early/mid" and not an all-clear: it's a suspension of the turn thesis pending confirmation. The physical cycle remains unambiguously late-and-hot (record sales, extended memory shortage, capex surge) — the debate was only ever about whether the *equity* had topped, and this week said "not yet / not confirmed."
- **Confidence: MEDIUM.** What supports the suspension: the ratio turned up, the macro headwind inverted on hard data (jobs), and the newest physical print (June sales) re-accelerated. What caps confidence and keeps me from calling a clean resumption: (1) the rally was **macro-driven risk-on**, the kind that produces sharp bear-market bounces as easily as durable bottoms; (2) **AMD still sold its beat**, so single-name "sell-the-good-news" hasn't cleared; (3) the ultimate arbiter — **NVDA (Aug 26)** — is unreported; and (4) the very catalyst (a weakening labor market) is a **double-edged** macro signal.
- **Key risk that could invalidate this view:** *Downside (re-confirms "turning")* — **NVDA (Aug 26) beats and gets sold** the way AMD/TSMC/SK Hynix/Samsung were, the SOX/SPX ratio rolls back over, and the weak-jobs relief curdles into hard-landing/demand-cut fear → the equity turn resumes and broadens. Also: an August CPI hot enough to un-do the dovish repricing (inflation data was flagged as "the deciding factor"). *Upside (would push toward a cleaner mid/late resumption)* — NVDA delivers a beat that **holds**, TSMC's July monthly (~Aug 10) re-accelerates, and the ratio extends its recovery → this week's snapback is confirmed as a durable valuation reset, not a bounce.
