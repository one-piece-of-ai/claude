## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Call maintained: LATE upcycle — TURNING. Confidence: medium (evidence this week tilted toward *confirming* the turn, but the decisive catalyst is still days away).** The 7/18 report set up hyperscaler capex (Jul 28–30) as the referee of whether the −20% SOX drawdown was a valuation reset within the upcycle or the cycle turning. Those prints haven't landed yet — but the market delivered a sharp preview and it leaned bearish. Semis **attempted the exact V-bounce that would have invalidated the turn**: Micron +10% Monday 7/20 on Morgan Stanley's raised memory targets, then **Intel posted its fastest revenue growth since 2011** (7/23) and **TI's data-center revenue doubled** (7/22) — blowouts from the cycle's *laggards*, confirming the upcycle is broadening, not narrowing. And the group **rejected all of it on Friday 7/24**: Intel −8% on its own beat, memory cratered (SK Hynix −7–11%, Micron −6%, SanDisk −8.6%, Kospi −5.7%) on a new MS warning that **memory contract prices peak in Q4 2026**, and the SOX round-tripped a ~5% intraweek bounce to close **+1.24% on the week at 11,818.89 — still −19% from its 6/22 peak.** Selling a blowout is now the market's *repeated* reflex (7/17 TSMC/ASML, 7/24 Intel). That is topping behavior, not a reset. **Hyperscaler capex Jul 28–30, SK Hynix 7/29, Samsung 7/30 and the FOMC 7/28–29 remain the arbiter — now days away.**

## 3. KEY SIGNALS INTERPRETATION

- **Demand momentum: peaking — and the peak signature repeated this week.** The single cleanest tell of a top is when the *best possible news can no longer move the stocks up.* It happened twice now: 7/17 (TSMC/ASML beat-and-raise, sold) and 7/24 (**Intel's fastest growth since 2011 → stock −8%**). Fundamentals didn't just hold this week, they *broadened* — Intel (the laggard) and TI (analog/industrial) both blew out — yet the group round-tripped its bounce. Accelerating, broadening fundamentals + a market that won't pay for them = momentum has peaked.
- **Supply tightness vs. oversupply: still tight now; the first crack in the *narrative* is a named memory peak.** DRAM/NAND remain undersupplied and capex is still being *raised* into the tightness (Intel >$20B, ASML €43–45B, TSMC $52–56B). But the market began pricing the *other side* of that capex surge: **Morgan Stanley's 7/21 call put a date on the memory crest — Q4 2026** — and TI flagged its own **analog supply "loosening even as demand broadens."** Top-of-cycle capex is being laid down exactly as the first "peak" timing gets published.
- **Memory cycle direction: cresting, now with a Q4-2026 date.** The controlling read sharpened this period. TrendForce's decelerating Q3 forecast (+13–18% DRAM / +10–15% NAND) is now paired with **MS's explicit Q4-2026 peak** for contract prices. Prices are still rising and supply is still tight; the *pace* is rolling over and a crest is being timed. Friday's memory rout (SK Hynix, Micron, SanDisk, Kospi −5.7%) was the market front-running that peak.
- **AI-driven demand strength vs. cyclical weakness: demand strong and broadening; the *valuation* keeps failing the test.** This week's demand news was unambiguously strong and *wider* than before — analog (TI data-center 2×), foundry (Intel +31%, 18A ahead), memory (MS raised targets). The weakness is entirely in price/positioning: profit-taking, "infrastructure overcapacity" fear, stretched multiples, and an Asian memory rout spilling into US names. The gap between accelerating fundamentals and a market that sells them is the definition of this juncture — and it *widened* this week rather than closing.

## 5. CYCLE POSITIONING CALL

- **Where we are: LATE upcycle — TURNING (maintained from 7/18).** Nothing this week argued for downgrading back to plain "LATE," and several things argued for firming the turn. The invalidation trigger the prior report specified — *semis V-bounce and reclaim leadership* — was **attempted (Mon–Thu, ~+5% off the low) and rejected (Fri −4.4%)**, closing the ratio essentially flat (~1.595) and still −19% from peak. Meanwhile the fundamental cycle not only held but *broadened* to its laggards (Intel, analog), and the market sold a second consecutive blowout. Stocks peaking while earnings still accelerate — and broaden — is the textbook of this junction.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM (unchanged, but the balance shifted toward the turn).** Turn case strengthened: the bounce failed, sell-the-news repeated on Intel, and a named Q4-2026 memory peak arrived from a memory *bull*. Not-yet-turned case still stands: fundamentals are blowout and *widening*, every capex guide is still going up, net weekly SOX was actually *up* +1.24%, and — decisively — **the real arbiter (hyperscaler capex Jul 28–30) has not printed.** Escalating to high confidence before that data would repeat the prior report's own discipline in reverse; the week is a strong tell, not a confirmation.
- **Key risk that could invalidate this view:** *Upside (invalidates the turn)* — hyperscalers (MSFT/META/GOOGL/AMZN, Jul 28–30) **raise** 2026 capex again with no digestion language, semis finally hold a bounce and reclaim leadership → revert to plain LATE, the drawdown was a valuation reset. *Downside (confirms "turned")* — any hyperscaler trims/defers capex or uses "digestion/overcapacity" language, SK Hynix (7/29) or Samsung (7/30) signal 2027 memory oversupply, or a hawkish FOMC (7/28–29) compresses multiples further into a group already −19% and priced for perfection.
