## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The cycle has taken its first real turn — but not the one we were watching for.** Last report's flagged risk (a memory-price rollover) did **not** materialize; the opposite happened. Micron blew past its own guide (FQ3 revenue **$41.5B vs. $33.5B guided**, GM **84.9% vs. 81%**, Q4 guided to **$50B**), and DRAM/NAND contract prices were still being marked *up* on 6/30. Fundamentals accelerated. **What broke instead was the tape:** the SOX **peaked 14,655 on June 22, then fell ~11% in a week** to ~12,633 and is now *lagging* the S&P 500 for the first time in this series — triggered by Broadcom's soft AI-chip guide (no FY26 raise), record smartphone demand destruction (–13% YoY, driven by high memory prices), and a hawkish rate shift. This is the classic late-cycle signature: **peak earnings + a rolling-over price.** Moving the call to **LATE upcycle, turning risk rising.**

## 3. KEY SIGNALS INTERPRETATION

- **Demand momentum: peaking, with the first cyclical crack downstream.** The core AI/memory pull is still accelerating (Micron blowout, contract prices up, hyperscaler capex now tracked at **~$680–740B for 2026**). But the **first demand-destruction evidence** appeared: high memory prices are crushing the smartphone market (**–13% YoY, largest on record**), and **Broadcom guided Q3 AI-chip sales to $16B vs. $17.2B expected and did not raise its FY26 AI forecast** — the first AI-guidance disappointment of this cycle. Demand is still strong at the core but no longer uniformly accelerating at the edges.
- **Supply tightness vs. oversupply: still acute tightness — no oversupply anywhere.** HBM is sold out through 2026 across all three makers; Samsung is adding ~50% capacity and SK Hynix >4x prior investment plans, but the 3:1 HBM-to-DDR5 wafer conversion keeps general DRAM structurally short. The binding constraint remains supply, not demand.
- **Memory cycle direction: still late-but-rising, NOT peaked.** Last report's caution — spot softening as the first domino — reversed: DDR4 spot rose to $35.90 and contract quotes were marked up again on 6/30. Micron's Q4 guide (revenue +20% QoQ to $50B, GM to ~86%) says the *pricing* peak is not yet in. The memory sub-cycle is the strongest leg here.
- **AI-driven demand vs. cyclical weakness: still AI-driven, but the market is now pricing digestion risk.** The SOX sell-off wasn't a fundamentals miss (Micron proved that) — it was **valuation + rate + the first ASIC-side guidance wobble (Broadcom)**. The read: demand is real, but the market has moved from "priced for perfection" to "questioning the slope," which is exactly how late-cycle tops begin to form.

## 5. CYCLE POSITIONING CALL

- **Where we are: LATE upcycle, turning risk rising (moved from MID-to-LATE).** Forward/capex indicators (ASML guide, TSMC, hyperscaler capex ~$680–740B) still read expansionary and 12–36 months of runway remain on paper. But the **highest-frequency leading indicator — SOX relative strength — has decisively rolled over and is now lagging the S&P**, and the first downstream demand cracks (smartphones, Broadcom) have appeared *simultaneously with peak earnings*. Price rolling over into peak fundamentals is the textbook late-cycle divergence.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM.** The bull leg is still concrete (Micron blowout, contract prices up, ASML/TSMC forward). The turn signal is one week of price action plus two demand data points — real, but not yet confirmation. A top is a process; this is the start of one, not proof of one.
- **Key risk that could invalidate this view (both directions):** *Bearish trigger* — if the SOX pullback deepens and bleeds into contract-price guidance or a hyperscaler capex cut, "turning" becomes "turned." *Bullish trigger* — if ASML Q2 bookings (July 15) come in very strong and the SOX pullback proves to be a healthy 11% consolidation after a 130% run, the read reverts toward a cleaner late-mid-cycle. The Broadcom miss vs. the Micron blowout is the tension to resolve.
