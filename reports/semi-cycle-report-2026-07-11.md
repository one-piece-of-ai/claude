## 3. KEY SIGNALS INTERPRETATION

- **Demand momentum: peaking (still rising, no longer accelerating everywhere).** SIA May sales accelerated to +104% YoY and hit a record — but that series is a 3-month moving average dominated by memory price inflation, so it *lags* the turn the leading indicators are flagging. The forward-looking reads are the tell: memory contract-price *increases* are moderating (TrendForce 7/3), MoM sales growth ticked down (+11%→+9.2%), and Samsung's record earnings couldn't lift its stock. Demand is at or near peak momentum, not building.
- **Supply tightness vs. oversupply: still acute in memory/HBM — but the supply *response* is being financed at the top.** DRAM/HBM remain sold out; CoWoS sold out through 2026. Two capital events this period are classic late-cycle behavior: **SK Hynix raised $26.5B in the largest-ever foreign US listing (Nasdaq debut 7/10, +14–15%, 7x oversubscribed)** and Korea's ₩392T package funds new HBM fabs. Strong demand signal *now*; the seeds of 2027+ oversupply are being planted with cheap top-of-cycle capital.
- **Memory cycle direction: cresting — first deceleration tell after three reports of "the rollover keeps not happening."** For the first time, TrendForce framed pricing as *moderating* (Q3 DRAM +13–18% vs. Q2 +63%) and named the mechanism: record contract prices have hit consumer affordability limits (PCs, smartphones). Prices are NOT falling — but the pace of increase is rolling over, which is how memory tops always begin. Combined with Samsung's -6–7% reaction to a record print, memory looks like it is putting in a profitability peak.
- **AI-driven demand strength vs. cyclical weakness: AI pillar re-firmed this week, partly reversing the 7/2 Meta scare.** Meta *raised* 2026 capex to $125–145B (from $115–135B) and rallied ~15% on the week (best since early 2024); NVDA +~4% Friday; hyperscaler 2026 capex tracked at ~$660–725B (+77% YoY). The 7/2 "excess-compute" digestion fear did not propagate into other names' guidance — the AI-capex bull case is intact for now. The cyclical weakness is concentrated in *consumer/memory-price-elastic* end demand, not AI.

## 5. CYCLE POSITIONING CALL

- **Where we are: LATE upcycle — topping process underway (unchanged from 7/4, evidence marginally firmer).** The fundamental cycle (billings, record sales, foundry, capex guides) is still expanding, and the AI pillar re-firmed this week. But the *leading and second-derivative* reads deteriorated further beneath the price bounce: memory price increases are now decelerating with an affordability ceiling named, a record Samsung print was sold, and semis failed to reclaim relative leadership even as the S&P returned to near-record highs. That combination — lagging aggregate data still rising while leading reads roll over — is the signature of a top forming, not a fresh leg.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM.** Genuinely two-sided. Bull side: market recovered, Meta raised capex, SK Hynix's mega-debut was 7x oversubscribed, contract prices are still rising, and TSMC/ASML could easily reignite the tape next week. Bear side: the Samsung sell-the-news and the memory-price deceleration are exactly the kind of *specific, fundamental* tells that mark memory/AI-capex peaks. Neither side has control — hence medium, not high.
- **Key risk that could invalidate this view:** *Upside* — a blowout ASML Q2 bookings print (7/15) confirming the 2027 backlog is fully booked, plus a strong TSMC June (7/13) and Jul 16 guide, would prove the 7/2 scare was a one-off and revert the call to plain LATE (or extend the cycle). *Downside* — a soft ASML bookings number, or any hyperscaler echoing Meta's excess-compute language in late-July earnings, would convert "topping process" to "turned," with the Q3-2026 hyperscaler-capex-exceeds-cash-flow crossover as the natural stress point.
