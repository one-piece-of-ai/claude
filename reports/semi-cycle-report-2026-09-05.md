## 3. KEY SIGNALS INTERPRETATION

- **Demand momentum: still accelerating — now DOUBLY confirmed (NVDA then Broadcom).** Broadcom's Q3 (Sep 2) put AI-semi revenue at **$16.7B, +221% YoY (56% of total revenue)**, on total revenue $29.6B (+86%), and raised the multi-year AI outlook to **$58B (FY26, +186%) → $115B (FY27) → $230B (FY28)** with a Q4 AI-semi guide of $21.7B (+236%), driven by **six XPU customers including Google, Anthropic and OpenAI**. Coming two weeks after NVDA's +106%/~70%-FY28-guide, this is a second, independent, blowout confirmation that AI/data-center demand is *not* peaking — and it lands on the **custom-ASIC (XPU) axis**, so both the GPU and the custom-ASIC legs of AI compute are inflecting up together, not trading share destructively. The lone blemish: AVGO's **Q4 revenue guide ($34.8B) came in a hair below the $35.03B consensus**, and the stock sold ~5% on the night — a reminder the bar is now extreme and even great prints can sell off. Demand is not the problem.
- **Supply tightness vs. oversupply: still tight (server/AI-driven); price momentum still decelerating; consumer soft.** TrendForce's Sep 2 read is the same trichotomy as the prior three reports: 3Q26 contract prices **finalized higher on server/AI demand**, PC DRAM contract still rising but with **narrowing gains** and a buyer/seller stalemate, and **spot flat/subdued** on weak consumer end-demand. Structural shortages (NOR, SLC NAND) persist into 2H26. No oversupply anywhere — but this remains a *decelerating-increase*, server-led tape, not a re-accelerating broad one. Consistent with late-cycle.
- **Memory cycle direction: physical shortage intact; momentum cresting; equities recovered with the group this week.** Micron (no new print) reiterates HBM **sold out for 2026**, ~20% bit-shipment growth in CY26, and industry supply "substantially short of demand for the foreseeable future," with an HBM TAM path of **$35B (2025) → ~$100B (2028), ~40% CAGR**. Physical shortage runway intact; the equity leg, which had been rolling over, bounced with the sector this week. Not a downturn in the physical data.
- **AI-driven demand strength vs. cyclical weakness: the week's lesson INVERTED last week's.** On 8/29, a blowout (NVDA) could not lift semis against a hawkish Fed — the de-rate was winning. **This week, semis rallied +3.4% on Friday straight through a hawkish jobs shock** (+162K, hike odds to ~60%+) on the back of a second blowout (AVGO). When the leading indicator turns UP *despite* the cost-of-capital vector moving further against it, the August de-rate looks more like a **rate-driven correction that demand leadership is beginning to overpower** than the first leg of a cyclical top. That is the counter-signal to the turn — hence the downgrade from "confirmed" to "on watch." The caveat: it is a single week, the ratio (~1.520) is still far below the ~1.98 June peak and the ~1.595 early-August level, and the **CPI/FOMC gauntlet** can re-impose the rate pressure fast.

## 5. CYCLE POSITIONING CALL

- **Where we are: LATE upcycle — turn call downgraded from CONFIRMED to ON WATCH / neutralized; physical/demand cycle intact and re-confirmed.** The 8/29 report confirmed an equity/leading-indicator turn on a three-week SOX/SPX breakdown to fresh lows plus a blowout (NVDA) that couldn't hold. **This week broke that trajectory:** the SOX/SPX ratio ticked **up** (1.487 → ~1.520, first weekly gain in a month), the SOX **rallied +3.4% straight through a hawkish jobs shock**, and a **second blowout AI print (Broadcom, AI +221%)** doubly confirmed demand across both GPU and custom-ASIC. When the leading indicator turns up *despite* the cost-of-capital vector moving further against it, the August de-rate reads more like a **rate-driven correction that demand leadership is starting to overpower** than the first leg of a cyclical top. Every physical read remains up (NVDA +106%, AVGO AI +221%, TSMC record, DRAM/HBM short). So this is still a **valuation/rate story inside an intact physical upcycle**, but the *equity-turn* conviction that had been building is now materially weaker.
- **Confidence: on the turn, LOW–MEDIUM (down from the 8/29 medium). On "late upcycle," unchanged (solid).** What weakened the turn: the single most important high-frequency signal (SOX/SPX) reversed higher and did so *through* a hawkish macro; the demand objection is now doubly refuted; the drawdown has narrowed back to ~-20% from >-21.7%. What keeps me from an all-clear: it is **one week**; the ratio (~1.520) is still far below the ~1.595 early-Aug and ~1.98 June-peak levels, so this is a bounce off a low, not a new high; and the **rate pressure is intensifying, not easing** — hike odds are >60% into a live FOMC. The driver remains macro/rates and therefore whipsaw-prone: a hot CPI or a hawkish FOMC could re-break the ratio within days, while a soft CPI would likely confirm the bounce and reopen the upcycle.
- **Key risk that could invalidate this view (both directions live):** *Bounce confirms (upside):* a **soft/in-line Aug CPI (~Sep 11)** and/or a Fed that hikes-and-signals-done at the **Sep 15–16 FOMC** caps the long end, the SOX/SPX ratio reclaims ~1.55–1.60, and the August de-rate is retroactively a "healthy correction" — upcycle resumes (demand already re-confirmed). *Turn re-asserts (downside):* **hot CPI** and/or a hawkish hike with more to come, the 30Y grinds back toward/above 5.3%, and the AI-capex-financing worry re-broadens (note AVGO's own guide-driven dip shows the bar is now punishing) — the ratio rolls back to new lows and this week is just a bear-market bounce. The next two weeks (CPI → FOMC) resolve it; this report deliberately does not over-extrapolate one week in either direction.

*(Kept separate from the objective analysis above.)* For a tech-heavy book with ~20% semi exposure (SMH/SOXX/NVDA direct + QQQM/VGT look-through) running covered calls: **stay the course — hold the current modestly-defensive tilt, but STOP leaning further into defense; do not re-risk yet either.** This week's evidence cuts against last week's "lean more defensive" instruction: the leading indicator (SOX/SPX) turned back up, semis decoupled from a hawkish jobs shock, and a second blowout AI print (Broadcom) doubly-confirmed demand — so getting *more* defensive here risks selling a bottom. But the macro gauntlet (**CPI 9/11, FOMC 9/15–16, hike odds >60%**) is imminent and whipsaw-prone, so re-risking ahead of it is equally premature. Net: **keep writing covered calls but ease back toward at-/slightly-out-of-the-money strikes rather than pressing closer-to-the-money into a rebound** (don't cap a recovering sector too tightly), **keep SOXL/leverage at zero** (too much macro-event risk in the next 10 days to add beta), **keep the core semi sleeve intact**, and let the **CPI → FOMC** outcome — not another earnings print — be the trigger to either re-risk (soft CPI / hike-and-done → add back beta, widen strikes) or tighten fully defensive (hot CPI / hawkish FOMC → strikes back in, raise cash). **Bottom line: neutral-to-modestly-defensive, zero leverage, core held, strikes eased off the body — a deliberate hold through the two-week macro gauntlet rather than a directional bet in either direction.**
