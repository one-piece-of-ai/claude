## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Call moves from LATE upcycle → LATE upcycle with a topping process now underway.** What changed vs. 6/27: the SOX sell-off did **not** stabilize — after a modest bounce June 29–July 1 (to 13,353), the index fell another **-5.4% on July 2 to 12,626**, now **-13.8% below its June 22 peak, on a day the S&P 500 was flat and the Dow hit a record high**. Critically, this second leg down had a **new, fundamental catalyst — Meta announced it will sell excess AI computing power as a cloud service**, the first hyperscaler signal that AI capacity may be outrunning internal demand; SK Hynix fell -14.5% and Samsung -9.1% on the news before rebounding +10%/+8% on July 3. Meanwhile the **fundamental data got stronger again**: TrendForce *raised* its Q3/Q4 memory contract-price forecasts (6/30), TSMC's FY26 guide was raised to >30% YoY growth, and Samsung/SK Hynix are set to report record Q2 profits July 7 / July 29. Fundamentals accelerating while price leadership breaks down on demand-durability fears is the textbook signature of a cycle top forming — the market is now trading the *end* of the capex boom while the boom itself is still strengthening.

## 3. KEY SIGNALS INTERPRETATION

- **Demand momentum: still accelerating at the core — but the market has found its first credible reason to doubt the slope.** Every fundamental series that updated this period got *better*: TrendForce raised Q3/Q4 memory price forecasts (6/30), TSMC raised FY26 guidance to >30% growth, Samsung/SK Hynix Q2 consensus was revised sharply higher (combined ~₩150T operating profit expected). The doubt is no longer generic "valuation" — **Meta signaling it has *excess* AI compute to resell is the first datapoint suggesting hyperscaler capacity may be getting ahead of internal demand**, and the market repriced the whole complex on it in one session.
- **Supply tightness vs. oversupply: still acute tightness in memory and advanced packaging.** Supplier DRAM inventories described as rock-bottom (TrendForce, 6/30); CoWoS "extremely tight and sold out through 2026" with packaging prices rising at 2–4x wafer rates (TSMC annual meeting, Jun 4); HBM sold out with ~20% HBM3E price hikes for 2026. No physical oversupply signal anywhere yet — but Korea's ₩392T investment package (Jul 2) and Samsung/SK Hynix HBM fab buildouts are the supply response that eventually ends every memory cycle, on a 2027+ fuse.
- **Memory cycle direction: still rising — the feared rollover keeps not happening.** For the third consecutive report, spot/contract prices refused to crack: contract quotes were marked up again and forward price forecasts were *raised*. The memory upcycle is intact and cresting at record profitability (Micron FQ4 guide ~86% GM). The risk has shifted from "prices roll over" to "prices are so high they destroy downstream demand" — smartphone units are now forecast to fall **-13% YoY in 2026, the largest decline on record**, driven by memory cost inflation.
- **AI-driven demand strength vs. cyclical weakness: the AI pillar itself is now being stress-tested.** Hyperscaler 2026 capex is tracked at ~$690–725B (+60%+ YoY), but two fragility markers emerged: (1) aggregate hyperscaler capex is on track to **exceed operating cash flow around Q3 2026** (Epoch AI) — the point where the buildout must be financed rather than self-funded; (2) Meta's compute-resale move. Add Broadcom's Q3 AI guide miss ($16B vs. $17.2B expected, no FY26 AI raise, mid/late June) and the pattern is: AI demand is real but no longer accelerating *everywhere at once*.

## 5. CYCLE POSITIONING CALL

- **Where we are: LATE upcycle — topping process underway (incrementally worse than 6/27's plain "LATE").** The fundamental cycle (billings, sales, memory pricing, foundry, capex guides) is still rising and in places accelerating. But the market's leading read has now put in **two distinct down-legs in two weeks — the second on a genuine demand-durability catalyst (Meta), not just valuation** — while the S&P makes new highs. Historically the SOX/SPX ratio leads the billings cycle; a -15% relative break in nine sessions against accelerating fundamentals says the market has begun pricing the peak, even though the peak in the data isn't in yet.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM.** Two-sided in a way that resists high conviction: the July 3 Korea rebound (+8–10%) shows the bears aren't in control either; memory contract prices, TSMC's raise, and record Samsung/SK Hynix earnings due next week could easily fuel one more leg up. What keeps this from being LOW confidence is the *quality* of the new bear evidence — a hyperscaler advertising surplus compute is the specific kind of signal that has historically marked AI-capex inflection risk, and it landed on top of an already-broken relative-strength trend.
- **Key risk that could invalidate this view:** *Upside invalidation* — ASML posts blowout Q2 bookings (Jul 15) and Samsung/SK Hynix record prints (Jul 7/29) restart the tape, proving July 2 was a one-day scare and the pullback a consolidation in an ongoing upcycle; the call would revert to plain LATE (or even extend). *Downside confirmation* — any second hyperscaler echoing Meta (capex trim, compute resale, digestion language) in July earnings would convert "topping process" to "turned," and the Q3-2026 capex-exceeds-cash-flow threshold makes late summer the natural stress point.
